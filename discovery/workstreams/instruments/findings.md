### Falsifiable stock-token mechanism card — INST-06

- **Possible spread cause/payer:** voluntary demand segmented by trading hours, issuer eligibility, custody, financing and chain inventory; the counterparty paying a premium is not a guaranteed protocol subsidy.
- **Required exact comparison:** same share class/exchange and currency; normalized multiplier/rebase-adjusted units; issuer entitlement; net-tax dividends, splits and other actions; actual primary-redemption rights; spot borrowability; named perp index/mark, funding, collateral and hours.
- **Financing lifecycle:** fund/borrow the actual token, maintain the separate hedge/margin, reconcile corporate actions and funding, close/redeem through an eligible documented path, repay liabilities, then release collateral. No completed accessible lifecycle was identified.
- **Reject cheaply if:** a leg is mock/testnet-only, a contract or market is missing, no participant can access both closing paths, economic units differ, or no defensible convergence mechanism exists. Only after those checks would synchronized executable quotes be useful.
- **Not locked convergence:** different issuers' promises cannot be delivered against one another merely because the ticker or reference stock matches. A cash-settled perpetual is another distinct claim. A correlated 60-day price chart would not establish enforceable conversion or net profitability.

Separately, Aave's changelog reports October 7, 2026 Base Equities hub/MAG7 spoke listing USDC and AAPLc/AMZNc/GOOGLc/METAc/MSFTc/NVDAc/TSLAc. These are **not automatically xStocks, Robinhood securities or borrowable stock liabilities**. No rights, permissions or live borrow state for those Aave-listed assets was verified. [Official changelog](https://aave.com/docs/resources/changelog).

## 1. Borrowing replaces timing inventory, not collateral — INST-01

The seed paper's **Appendix G** gives a concrete historical sequence: acquire WBTC on Ethereum, start the bridge to Arbitrum, borrow WBTC locally through Aave while that transfer is pending, complete the destination trade, and repay when bridged WBTC arrives. The paper reports this as an observed loan-backed example, not a future atomic cross-chain flash-loan interface. Its empirical window is **September 2023–August 2024**; the v2 revision date is June 18, 2025. We read the case and its paper-provided transaction links, but did not independently replay those transactions or adopt its stated profit as a current/net result. [Seed Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17335v2#A7).

Aave credit delegation does not remove collateral from the system: the delegator must supply collateral before the delegatee borrows; eMode and health-factor constraints still apply. Delegation concerns who may borrow against the supplier's position and who bears the debt. Current collateral haircuts, borrow caps, utilization, reserve identities, interest and legal access must be checked before treating the idea as feasible. [Aave Credit Delegation](https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/guides/credit-delegation).

**Economic hypothesis:** ordinary cross-chain liquidity segmentation may pay for faster locally available inventory. Required capital includes collateral, liquidation buffer, the other leg's inventory, gas and failure reserves. Borrowing WBTC does not let a tBTC receipt repay that debt without a valid conversion. A bridge delay can leave a collateralized loan outstanding after the trading opportunity disappears.

**Cheapest falsifier:** identify one exact currently borrowable destination reserve and matching repayable bridge receipt; reject immediately if unavailable or if collateral plus inventory consumes the capital constraint. No present-day route was checked here.

## 2. A real 2026 cost change: stake-dependent tBTC fees — INST-02

Threshold's official fee-waiver announcement is dated **January 22, 2026**, and currently includes a retrospective note that the mint fee returned to 20 bps on **April 15, 2026**. Its current text describes mint/redemption fees of 20 bps each, up to **0.001 tBTC of eligible fee offset per 100,000 T staked per rolling 30 days**, and **30-day unstaking**. This is product documentation, not an independently read current contract setting. The January publication date must not be treated as an immutable snapshot of the later-edited page. [Official announcement](https://www.threshold.network/blog/tbtc-redemption-fee-waivers-for-t-stakers).

This creates a plausible **cost-floor segmentation** rather than a free yield: an eligible active participant might convert BTC/tBTC at a lower protocol-fee cost, but locks T capital, retains or hedges its price exposure, and still pays non-waived fees, Bitcoin mining cost, chain costs, slippage, financing and rebalancing. Fee savings require qualifying turnover and remaining waiver capacity; no spread, turnover or net return was measured.

Technical redemption is asynchronous: requested BTC output is reduced by treasury/Bitcoin transaction fees, requests have wallet/dust/output constraints, and completion uses Bitcoin SPV proof. The old API page also describes timeout token return; that is not assured immediate native BTC payment. Live timeout/fee/wallet parameters were not queried. [Redemption API](https://docs.threshold.network/app-development/tbtc-contracts-api/tbtc-v2-api/redemption).

The official registry lists Ethereum tBTC/Bridge, Arbitrum/Base variants, a **Solana mint** and a **Sui coin type**; exact identifiers are in the mechanism card. These are documented deployment identifiers, not independently checked code, supply, channel state or current liquidity. The ecosystem guide distinguishes direct minting from bridged access and reports **May 12, 2026** Ekubo migration from tBTC/WBTC to **strkBTC/tBTC on Starknet**. That is a useful in-window non-EVM claim-structure lead, but the second token's redemption/privacy/issuer rights were not reviewed and cannot be equated with tBTC or native BTC. The guide's displayed May publication date coexists with later June content, and its embedded APY/TVL images were not independently decoded or used as returns. [Registry](https://docs.threshold.network/contract-addresses/tbtc), [2026 ecosystem guide](https://www.threshold.network/blog/tbtc-opportunities-in-2026-bitcoin-vaults-pools-lending-markets-and-institutional-pathways).

**Cheapest falsifier:** current waiver capacity and eligibility, the exact representation-to-native-BTC route, and locked T capital/hedge cost. If capacity or redemption access is inadequate, collecting a long price series is premature.

## 3. Naturally constrained bridge inventory, not a capacity bounty — INST-03

Wormhole NTT documents source outbound and destination inbound caps, continuously replenishing capacity over a configurable window, queued delivery and reverse-flow capacity replenishment. Its default window is documented as 24 hours, **not a universal deployment parameter**. Queued sending/receiving may require users/relayers to return after the queue duration. "Cancel flows" refers to opposing flow refilling rate-limit capacity, **not** an unconditional transfer refund/cancellation or protocol payment to a trader. The rendered page says created/updated October 28, 2026; this is not an NTT launch date. [NTT rate limiting](https://wormhole.com/docs/products/token-transfers/native-token-transfers/configuration/rate-limiting/).

**Hypothesis:** genuine one-way demand exhausting a live permitted route could make available destination inventory valuable to voluntary counterparties. No production token/route with such demand, paying service margin or bounty was established; the source's Sepolia configuration example is not production evidence. No induced congestion, capacity draining or manipulation is part of this idea.

**Cheapest falsifier:** no genuinely constrained mainnet route with a willing paying counterparty, or any natural inventory premium being smaller than all carry/rebalance costs.

## 4. Exact representations and route costs matter — INST-04

LayerZero OFT requires source debit (burn/lock), verified messaging and destination credit (mint/unlock) through paired directional channels. Optional composing executes in a **new destination transaction**, not a global atomic rollback. Shared-decimal normalization/dust, adapter escrow, fees, roles and custom accounting for rebasing/fee-on-transfer assets must be understood. Equal token names or equal global supply do not prove equal legal redemption rights or a profitable price difference. [OFT technical reference](https://docs.layerzero.network/v2/concepts/technical-reference/oft-reference).

Tempo's mainnet-labeled documentation is a concrete representation/fee example: it distinguishes **USDC.e** issued through Stargate liquidity infrastructure from native USDC/native issuance, and distinguishes the Stargate interface from issuer-standard OFTs such as USDT0. It documents **0 bps Stargate transfer fee on Ethereum↔Tempo**, with other routes quote-dependent. That does **not** mean zero execution/messaging cost. Tempo outbound messaging uses **LZEndpointDollar**, a TIP-20 fee wrapper. Registry addresses, endpoint ID and security-OApp distinction are documented but not independently queried here. The displayed update string is `10/11/2026, 04:13 PM`; its locale and actual instrument launch dates were not established. [Tempo bridge documentation](https://tempo.xyz/developers/docs/guide/bridge-layerzero).

**Cheapest falsifier:** there is no accessible conversion back to the exact closing asset, or an apparent basis vanishes after share/decimal normalization, route-specific messaging and financing/rebalancing costs. OUSD, USDC.e, USDT0 and yield-bearing claims are not interchangeable.

## 5. New collateral markets are discovery leads, not universal short access — INST-05

Aave's official changelog reports V4 Ethereum mainnet on **March 30, 2026**, Avalanche on **July 15**, Arc on **September 16**, and Base's equities market on **October 7**. These dates fall inside the requested recent window. The same protocol's earlier deployments or brand age do not establish the risk age of a newly deployed version, market or issuer claim. A governance proposal is also not a deployment; only the changelog's reported deployments are listed here. [Official changelog](https://aave.com/docs/resources/changelog).

Admission as collateral does not imply an asset can be borrowed, shorted or delivered into another venue. The Threshold guide makes a related architecture distinction: Compound v3 collateral assets are not the base borrow/supply asset. A token may enable borrowing **against** it without enabling borrowing **of** it. [Threshold guide](https://www.threshold.network/blog/tbtc-opportunities-in-2026-bitcoin-vaults-pools-lending-markets-and-institutional-pathways).

**Cheapest falsifier:** the exact issuer claim is collateral-only, permissioned/ineligible, or has no matched closing liability. Full caps, oracles, legal rights and market-depth checks remain absent.
