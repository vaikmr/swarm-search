# Capacity: what a new $50k entrant can actually access

**Stage 0/1, as of 2026-10-29 UTC. No deployment recommendation.**
All capacity and target arithmetic below is **T4**. Public documentation is
reference evidence about mechanics/admission, not T1 realized PnL. No qualifying
T1 actor reconciliation, T2 competition-adjusted replay or T3 live paper period
has been established by this workstream. Unknown capture is not zero actual
profit, and public volume is not attainable profit.

## Bottom line

The first bottleneck is **eligible, executable flow after incumbents**, not total
protocol volume or the minimum hardware required to run sample software.
Self-funding a CoW bond fails the capital constraints; DAO-backed admission is a
different route. Across and UniswapX include exclusive flow. 1inch admission
depends on delegated network power and accepted access, not a known small cash
bond. The passive/yield candidates avoid some race bottlenecks but still need
eligible, look-through-compliant allocations and net return evidence.

**No positive entrant profit or win share can currently be certified.** That is
an evidence gate, not a prediction that every opportunity earns zero. If an
operator spends infrastructure money while winning no executable orders, cash
PnL is negative; lack of demonstrated capture must never be replaced by a
positive modeled market share.

## 1. Literal capital constraints

Inputs come from the [research charter](../docs/CHARTER.md). Reproducible T4
outputs: [bounds.json](capacity_sources/bounds.json), generated by
[calculate_bounds.py](capacity_sources/calculate_bounds.py).

- Total equity is **$50,000**; each shared failure domain is capped at **$15,000**
  by the **30%** rule.
- At most **three chains** accommodate at most **$45,000 on-chain**. At least
  **$5,000** remains outside those chain buckets: genuinely off-chain cash or
  otherwise unallocated. A stablecoin on a fourth chain is not cash outside the
  chain limit. A CEX balance remains a concentrated counterparty exposure.
- **$2,000-$5,000 net/month** requires **4%-10%** of total capital monthly.
  If the entire **$500/month** infrastructure ceiling were spent, the strategy
  would need **$2,500-$5,500 before that fixed cost**, plus any variable costs not
  already deducted. On the maximum **$45,000 on-chain** deployment that is
  **5.56%-12.22% monthly before variable costs**. These are requirements, not
  observed returns.
- The chain, stablecoin, bridge and protocol ceilings apply simultaneously.
  Dividing funds among addresses, vaults or chains does not split a shared
  issuer/protocol failure domain.

### Look-through ledger, not a list of addresses

For every position, record equity, free inventory, encumbered margin, redemption
claims, pending reimbursements, bridge receivables, issuer/wrapper, protocol
control and chain dependencies. Sum at-risk equity and obligations for every
common failure that can cause loss or prevent a hedge exit. Count idle inventory
and claims, not just the amount traded. Do not count margin or refunds as
simultaneously available lending capital.

Examples of necessary aggregation:

- USDC on multiple chains remains one issuer exposure; a USDC-backed derivative
  does not automatically create a new independent stablecoin bucket.
- A Pendle PT remains exposed to Pendle, its yield-bearing underlying, the
  underlying stablecoin/issuer, wrappers and the chain. Redemption in underlying
  units is not a guaranteed USD principal floor.
- Across destination inventory already paid out becomes an unsettled protocol
  receivable. Count that with the same relayer's other outstanding Across
  receivables, not as freed reusable cash.
- DEX-held LP and lending-vault positions can share a chain, oracle, issuer or
  administrative dependency. A hedge does not remove the operational failure
  that prevents accessing it.
- A single CEX holding spot and margin is a common counterparty. Apply the
  no-single-point-of-failure principle rather than silently exempting the CEX.
- Shared L1 settlement or canonical-bridge dependencies can defeat apparent L2
  diversification. Their materiality is a red-team question; neither assume
  independence nor assert every L2 event causes total common loss.

The arithmetic cap is only a necessary condition. A **$15,000** complete-domain
loss still exceeds the **$5,000** monthly loss limit. Neither this allocation
bound nor a short profitable replay proves the hard **99% monthly** risk
requirement.

## 2. Capture model: separate every restrictive step

For each candidate, use:

1. **Visible opportunity** at the information and timestamp actually available.
2. **Eligible flow**: allowed chains/assets, age/TVL/audit gates, order size,
   admission, exclusivity and access rights.
3. **Executable flow**: inventory, margin, available gas, settlement state,
   order expiry, liquidity, slippage and hedge availability.
4. **Demonstrably won flow** after realistic detection-to-execution latency and
   competitor effects. Lose ties and every race that cannot be proven won.
5. **Realized net**: inventory/hedge marks and all variable costs, fixed
   infrastructure and separately reconciled benchmark opportunity cost.

Incumbent realized PnL can identify a lead, but does not grant its flow,
relationships, fee tier, capital, latency, hedging account or builder access to
this entrant. Neither `1 / number_of_bots` nor an arbitrary fraction of total
volume is an acceptable win-share assumption. Do not sum two candidates' capture
when they seek the same fills, inventory or infrastructure.

For a simplified inventory-fronting system:

```text
entrant turnover <= min(eligible executable won notional,
                        capital-time throughput bound)

net PnL = after-variable-cost margin * won turnover - fixed costs
```

The capital-time bound is a fluid upper bound only when inventory is fully
utilized, reusable after the assumed lock and continuously replenished. Real
event-level replay must enforce every balance and receivable transition.
Average latency and a nominal refund cadence are insufficient.

## 3. Candidate-by-candidate capacity screen

This is a feasibility screen and evidence request, **not the independent judge's
verdict**. Protocol age/TVL/audit eligibility and all positive returns remain
unverified unless the owning evidence package proves them.

| Candidate | New entrant bottleneck | What must be measured before assigning capture | CEX needed? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lending benchmark | Simultaneous protocol/chain/issuer limits; vault look-through, supply caps and withdrawal liquidity | Feasible allocation, time-aligned rate/share-price history, executable entry/exit costs, underlying eligibility | No |
| Across / cross-chain relaying | Exclusive nominations, route-specific inventory, outstanding repayments and rebalancing; **$15k** maximum aggregate Across at-risk exposure under the common-domain rule | Open/step-in flow actually reachable, nominated-flow access if any, order sizes, every fill-to-refund duration and fee, failed races | No |
| Inventory-based cross-chain DEX arbitrage | Non-atomic legs, inventory depletion and replenishment, overlapping asset/chain exposure; no bridge in hot path is not no bridge risk | Synchronized executable bid/ask, both-leg fills, marks on failed/partial legs, observed incumbent timing and rebalancing cost | No |
| Long-tail DEX arbitrage | Small pools, token/exit risk, low useful depth; many proposed niches fail age/TVL/audit gates | Eligible token/pool list, executable post-fee size, competitor inclusion and adverse inventory mark; exclude exploit/manipulation paths | No |
| Backrunning / order-flow auctions | Auction-specific user refunds, builder bids, limited hints, latency and incumbent competition | Own eligible information, net surplus after refunds/bids/gas, realistic ordering/inclusion; simulation profitability alone is insufficient | No |
| Smaller-market liquidations | Oracle/update visibility, keeper/backstop competition, capital or flash-liquidity access, collateral resale | Actual borrower eligibility at arrival state, safe debt/collateral prices, failed-race costs, post-sale net recovery, incumbent timing | No |
| CoW / UniswapX / 1inch solving | Bond/admission/access rights differ substantially; delegated admission is not automatic | Accepted access or explicit closed gate, candidate-specific rewards/locks, executable residual flow, latency and competitor fills | Optional for hedging |
| Delta-neutral funding/basis | Fully paid spot plus segregated perp margin; sign changes, liquidation liquidity, transfer/withdrawal interruptions | Signed realized funding, spot/basis marks, hedge opening/unwind costs, collateral path, margin drawdowns and venue limits | Flag if used; optional |
| Hedged concentrated LP | Active liquidity share, range occupancy, inventory drift, hedge costs/margin and common protocol exposures | Tick-level fees and holdings, all-in NAV versus matched benchmark, hedge execution and liquidation path; not fee APR alone | Optional |
| Fixed/variable yield / Pendle | Locked maturity exposure, executable pre-maturity exits and stacked underlying dependencies | Underlying redemption/share-price path, PT/market depth, actual rate changes and opportunity cost; no points assumed as cash | No |
| Keeper / automation bounties | Compensated operator admission, job eligibility, existing execution rotation and lumpy backlog | Permission to receive rewards, actual jobs available after competition, gas/fee net, capital-time and operator costs | No |
| CEX-DEX arbitrage | Both-venue inventory, base entrant fee tier, asynchronous legs, withdrawal freeze and speed incumbents | Actual venue permission/fees, synchronized depth, both-leg certainty, failed hedge marks and measured race advantage | **Yes** |

### Relaying: low software hardware minimum is not economic entry

Across's [V3 running guide][ACROSS_RUN] describes modest minimum hardware, but
also inventory rebalancing and chain RPC requirements. [Nomination docs][ACROSS_NOMINATION]
describe exclusive relayers and a performance-based entry process. The
implementation wording includes an older rollout schedule, so it is not a
current count of accessible flow.

The [lifecycle][ACROSS_LIFECYCLE] separates delivering funds from repayment:
validation, bundle proposal, challenge, cross-chain root execution and refund
come later. Its roughly **1.5-hour minimum bundle proposal interval** is
documentation, not a measured end-to-end repayment time. Never turn it into
instant capital recycling or assume a protocol upgrade preserves V3 timing.

### Solvers: distinguish self-bonding, sponsorship and public filling

**CoW self-funded route:** [official bond documentation][COW_BONDS] gives
**$500,000 plus 1,500,000 COW** for an independent pool. The reduced route starts
at **$50,000 plus 500,000 COW**, then grows. The stablecoin/ETH component alone
fails this study's **$15,000 protocol exposure cap**; the initial reduced pool
also exhausts the entire capital budget before acquiring COW or funding gas.
No COW price assumption is needed for that exclusion.

**CoW DAO-backed route:** [onboarding][COW_ONBOARD] is separate: company/KYC,
testing, accepted admission and managed settlement arrangements. The guide says
start on BNB, and describes **15% of weekly COW rewards** as a service fee
beginning after **six months**; the onboarding call also addresses locking
**25% of weekly rewards**. Weekly reimbursements imply a working-capital
requirement. Do not impose the self-bond on every solver, but do not assume
free instant sponsorship either. The BNB-first step needs explicit scope
treatment against the brief's exclusion; solver activity and builder-dominated
arbitrage should not be conflated.

**UniswapX:** the [retrieved current guide][UNISWAPX_FILL] distinguishes vetted
quoters from permissionless non-exclusive fillers. Public endpoint access does
not make RFQ exclusivity ours. The API is cached/capped and limited to **4
requests/second**. Our [single probes](capacity_sources/api_probes.json)
returned **zero** open orders on Base and Arbitrum at the recorded timestamps;
that is neither a negative profitability result nor a seven-day paper study.

**1inch:** the [August 2026 terms][ONEINCH_CURRENT] retain the documented
**5% total Unicorn Power delegation** threshold and at most **10 whitelisted
resolvers**; proxies are discussed where applicable/enabled. That is an
admission barrier, not a verified USD bond cost. No delegation, proxy commercial
agreement or accepted exclusive API access was obtained. Older timing/SLA
figures in legacy terms are not treated as verified current requirements.

### Race strategies: default capture stays unproven

[Flashbots troubleshooting][FLASHBOTS_COMPETITION] explicitly distinguishes
valid simulation from inclusion: competitors, bids and arrival timing can
exclude a profitable simulated bundle. The study's Ethereum L1/BSC exclusion
cannot be overridden by a successful sample arbitrage calculation. A
contestable niche needs positive evidence.

[Morpho's bot index][MORPHO_BOTS] lists both maintained and community liquidators.
That does not count profitable competitors, but it defeats the assumption that
a smaller lending market is automatically unattended. Flash liquidity can
reduce prefunding; it does not remove access, fees, inclusion or collateral
resale constraints.

### Yield strategies: capacity is easier to buy than net risk-adjusted yield

[Hyperliquid fees][HL_FEES] show base tier-0 perps **0.045% taker / 0.015% maker**
and spot **0.070% taker / 0.040% maker**. These are documentation prices, not
incurred costs. Do not give the entrant incumbent volume/staking discounts.
[Funding][HL_FUNDING] can change sign and is settled hourly; the interest
component alone is not a return forecast.

A conditional T4 sizing bound with fully funded spot notional `N`, perp leverage
`L <= 2`, and margin `N/L` is:

```text
N + N/L <= available paired-position equity
N/L <= single perp venue collateral cap
```

Using the maximum **$45,000** on-chain equity and **$15,000** initial perp margin
cap yields at most **$30,000 matched spot/short notional** at **2x** perp
leverage **for a single-perp-venue construction**. It is not a global bound on
all possible multi-venue funding portfolios. This is not proof a fully compliant three-chain allocation exists:
spot wrappers, chain placement, protocol common losses, margin buffers and
perp PnL can tighten it.

At that maximum, receiving **$2,500-$5,500** before fixed infrastructure costs
requires **8.33%-18.33% of matched notional in monthly funding receipts before
trading, hedge and basis costs**. Those are hurdle rates, not observed funding.
Spot and perp notional cannot each independently earn the same quoted APY on
all portfolio capital.

[Concentrated-liquidity docs][UNISWAP_CL] explain why an out-of-range position
stops earning fees and can become one asset. Position count does not diversify
the failure domain. [Pendle's FAQ][PENDLE_FAQ] describes maturity redemption in
underlying units and third-party risk, not risk-free dollars.

[Automation architecture][CHAINLINK_ARCH] describes registration and compensated
node work, but this is not evidence of an entrant's accepted paid node
participation or a freely available backlog. A developer registering a job is
not proof that the developer can collect operator revenue.

## 4. Required turnover and capital-lock sensitivity

All inputs in this section are **hypothetical T4 sensitivities**, not measured
spreads, lock times, achievable fill shares or forecasts. `Margin` means what is
left **after every variable cost**, including failed-race and rebalance costs
allocated to won volume. The **$500** fixed cost is the budget-ceiling scenario,
not planned or authorized spend.

| Assumed after-variable-cost margin | Turnover for $2k net + $500 fixed cost | Turnover for $5k net + $500 fixed cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 basis point | $25,000,000/month | $55,000,000/month |
| 2 basis points | $12,500,000/month | $27,500,000/month |
| 5 basis points | $5,000,000/month | $11,000,000/month |
| 10 basis points | $2,500,000/month | $5,500,000/month |

For one **$15,000** at-risk protocol sleeve and a **30-day/720-hour** month,
the ideal fluid `capital * hours / lock_hours` bound is:

| Hypothetical capital lock | Ideal turnover, before any flow/competition limitation |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 hour | $10,800,000/month |
| 6 hours | $1,800,000/month |
| 24 hours | $450,000/month |

The one-hour case is **not** an Across estimate. These bounds ignore finite
order flow, idle time, wrong-chain inventory, lock tails, disputed refunds,
discrete ordering and rebalancing, all of which reduce usable throughput.
No throughput bound establishes a positive margin.

For the Stage-2 hurdle, let `B_net` be the cost accountant's feasible
same-window lending benchmark. Required after-variable-cost turnover is:

```text
V_required = (B_net + $300 + fixed_strategy_costs) / positive_net_variable_margin
```

Do not compute this as a current numeric threshold while `B_net` or relevant
costs are unknown. For comparisons on the total portfolio, add genuine
non-overlapping idle-cash yield once, and subtract the same-window benchmark
once. Never earn lending yield on locked refunds or hedge margin.

## 5. Infrastructure: budget buys capacity, not latency advantage

[Alchemy's public price page][ALCHEMY_PRICE] lists a free **30 million CU/month**
plan at **15 RPS** and PAYG compute at **$0.52/million CU**. Method weights,
archive/traces, peak throughput, fallback providers, storage and servers still
matter. This work did not buy or use a paid plan.

T4 workload illustration: polling **three chains once/second** for a **30-day**
month means **7,776,000 requests**. At a hypothetical **10 CU/request**, PAYG
compute alone is **$40.44/month**; at **100 CU/request**, it is
**$404.35/month**, before servers, extra methods and redundancy. These are not
measured request weights or invoices; see the exact unrounded outputs.

The [MEV-Share rate-limit guide][FLASHBOTS_LIMITS] documents **1,800 bundle
submissions/minute** and **300 simulations/minute** for its named methods.
[Hyperliquid limits][HL_LIMITS] document **1,200 REST weight/minute** and
**100 public EVM RPC requests/minute**, with method weights and additional
address/congestion rules. None is a sub-block latency SLA. Access to more
requests does not prove the entrant beats a colocated or privileged competitor.

For each survivor, record public endpoint measurements of source timestamp,
request send/receive time, chain head age and missing/rejected observations.
The replay must worsen detection-to-execution latency by the specified
**50 ms/200 ms**, not merely delay an already-known profitable fill.
Gas and competitor stress remain separate scenario inputs.

## 6. Information required before promoting any candidate

- **Scouts:** reconciled actor/contract identity, fee payer, initial/final
  inventory and capital-time, winning fills, competitor evidence and a complete
  eligible-flow window. A registry file count is not active competitors.
- **Simulators:** preserve every rejection, unknown race, stale quote,
  unavailable hedge and tied fill; impose every outstanding balance/claim and
  shared exposure limit at each event. Report the portion of observations for
  which competition is unknown.
- **Accountant:** apply the entrant's actual tier and cost inclusions; reconcile
  cash net versus benchmark excess, idle inventory yield and capital locks.
- **Red team:** evaluate common failures, non-atomic exits and collateral needs,
  rather than assuming spot/perp neutrality prevents forced losses.
- **Judge:** keep access infeasibility, economic underperformance and missing
  evidence as different outcomes. Only actual qualifying T2 + T3 can support
  promotion; this file does not supply either.

Next useful comparison is between a feasible low-operational-complexity lending
allocation and any execution candidate that can first demonstrate accessible
flow and a positive competition-adjusted net margin. Until then the
**$2k-$5k/month target is unsupported**, not an estimated likely outcome.
